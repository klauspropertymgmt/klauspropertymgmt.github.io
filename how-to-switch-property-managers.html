How to switch property managers in Texas

Step by step, without upsetting your tenant or losing the deposit trail.

Unhappy with your current manager? Switching is usually simpler than owners expect. Here's how to do it cleanly.

1. Read your current management agreement

  • Find the termination section: how much notice is required, and whether there's a cancellation fee.
  • Check whether the old manager claims a fee on the current tenant's lease or renewals.

2. Give written notice

Send notice the way the agreement requires, and set a clear handover date, ideally right after a rent cycle.

3. Get your records and money

  • The tenant's security deposit, with a written record of the amount.
  • The signed lease, addenda, application and move-in inspection photos.
  • Keys, garage remotes, gate codes and HOA contacts.
  • A final owner statement and any reserve balance.

4. Tell your tenant

Your tenant needs to know who to pay, how to request repairs and who holds the deposit. A friendly letter from the new manager keeps it smooth.

How we make it easy

  • We handle the handover checklist with your old manager for you.
  • No leasing fee on a tenant already in place.
  • We review the existing lease and add anything missing at renewal.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Texas law changes, so check with an attorney before acting on a specific situation.
