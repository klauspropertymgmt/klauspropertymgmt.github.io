Dallas short-term rental rules: what owners should know

Where the Dallas rules stand, HOA limits, and hotel occupancy taxes.

Short-term rentals can earn more than a 12-month lease, but they come with more rules. In Dallas, those rules have been in court.

Where the Dallas rules stand

In 2023 Dallas adopted ordinances that would ban short-term rentals in single-family zoning and require registration. Owners sued, and a court blocked the city from enforcing them while the case continues. Dallas's appeals have so far been unsuccessful, so as of our last update short-term rentals can keep operating while the lawsuit plays out.

That could change with a final ruling. Before buying or converting a home for short-term use, check the current status and plan for what you'd do if the ban took effect, such as switching to furnished mid-term stays.

Your HOA may decide for you

Many condo and HOA communities ban stays under 30 days or set minimum lease terms, no matter what the city allows. Read your declaration and rules before listing.

Suburbs have their own rules

Irving, Fort Worth, Arlington and other DFW cities each have their own registration, zoning or permit rules. Check the city where the property sits.

Hotel occupancy taxes

  • Texas charges a 6% state hotel occupancy tax on stays under 30 days, and many cities and counties add their own.
  • Booking platforms collect some of these taxes but not always all of them, so you may still need to register and file.
  • Stays of 30 or more consecutive days are generally exempt, which is one reason furnished mid-term rentals are popular.

How we handle it

  • We check zoning, city rules and your HOA for your exact address before you spend anything.
  • We set up tax registration and filing so nothing is missed.
  • If rules change, we'll help you switch to mid-term or long-term renting.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Texas law changes, so check with an attorney before acting on a specific situation.
