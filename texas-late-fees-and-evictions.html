Texas late fees and the 2026 eviction changes

When you can charge a late fee, how much, and the 2026 eviction rule changes.

Collecting rent on time is the core of good management. Texas sets clear limits on late fees, and the eviction process changed for cases filed on or after January 1, 2026.

When a late fee can be charged

  • The late fee must be written in the lease (§ 92.019).
  • Rent must remain unpaid two full days after the due date. For rent due on the 1st, the earliest a late fee can apply is the 4th.

How much you can charge

  • Buildings with 4 or fewer units (most houses, condos and duplexes): up to 12% of the monthly rent is presumed reasonable.
  • Buildings with more than 4 units: up to 10%.

Going higher means proving your actual damages, so most leases stay at or under these amounts.

What changed for evictions in 2026

New rules took effect for eviction cases filed on or after January 1, 2026. A few that matter most to owners:

  • If the tenant has never been behind on rent before, the landlord must first give a notice to pay or vacate, giving them the chance to pay, instead of a plain notice to vacate.
  • Notices can now be delivered more ways, including inside the home in a visible spot, by hand to any occupant 16 or older, or electronically if the lease allows it.
  • Court timelines are shorter. The citation can be served as little as 4 days before trial, and tenants who appeal must pay rent into the court registry.

Eviction is always a last resort. Most late rent is solved with fast follow-up, a clear payment plan, and screening that prevents the problem in the first place.

How we handle it

  • Late fees written into every lease, within Texas limits.
  • Reminders before rent is due, and same-week follow-up when it's late.
  • If it ever comes to an eviction, the correct notice for the situation, coordinated with an attorney, and only with your OK.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Texas law changes, so check with an attorney before acting on a specific situation.
