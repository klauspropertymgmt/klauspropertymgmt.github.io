Long-term vs. mid-term vs. short-term rentals in Dallas

How to choose the rental strategy that earns the most for your home.

The same Dallas condo can earn very different amounts depending on how it's rented. Here's how the three strategies compare, and how to choose.

Long-term (12+ month leases)

  • Best for: houses, homes in good school zones, owners who want steady, low-touch income.
  • Pros: lowest turnover, tenants pay most utilities, simplest rules.
  • Cons: lowest gross rent, and you're tied to one tenant for the lease term.

Mid-term (furnished, 30+ nights)

  • Best for: condos and townhomes near hospitals, corporate offices and the airport, such as Oak Lawn, Uptown and Las Colinas.
  • Pros: often higher rent than a 12-month lease, and stays of 30+ days are generally exempt from hotel occupancy taxes.
  • Cons: you furnish the home, pay utilities and internet, and handle more turnovers.

Short-term (nightly)

  • Best for: larger homes or standout properties near attractions and event venues, where rules allow it.
  • Pros: highest income potential, and you can block dates for your own use.
  • Cons: most work and cost, hotel taxes, seasonal swings, and changing city rules.

How to choose

  1. Check the rules first. Your HOA and city may rule out short or mid-term stays.
  2. Compare net income, not rent. Subtract furnishing, utilities, cleaning, taxes and management for each option.
  3. Be honest about risk. Nightly income swings more. A steady lease may be worth more to you than a higher average.
  4. Keep a backup plan. A furnished home can switch between mid-term and short-term stays if rules or demand change.
